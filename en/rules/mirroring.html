Rule type in matrix reasoning tests

Mirroring

How the rule works

The third cell in every row is the first one mirrored: what sat on the left sits on the right, or what was on top is at the bottom. Everything flips at once, the lines along the edge, the symbols in a container, the dots on either side of a line.

A mirroring is not a rotation. A turned L and a mirrored L look different, and the options in such a task often differ in exactly that. Find something that breaks the symmetry, a single line or a dot, and see where it ends up.

Rules in the example

  • The third cell in every row is the first one with the symbols in reverse order.
  • Everything else is the same in every cell.

Level 2 in the app. The same example is shown in the app after a wrong answer on this rule. Rules in the example: Mirroring.

How to recognise it

  1. Compare cell one and three in the first row. Is three a mirror image of one?
  2. Decide the mirror axis: do left and right swap, or up and down?
  3. Mirror the first cell of the last row the same way. Do not confuse it with a turn.
