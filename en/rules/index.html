Fifteen rule types with solved examples

The rules in a matrix reasoning test.

Every task in a matrix reasoning test is governed by one or more rules. There are not endlessly many; there are fourteen. Anyone who recognises them does not have to search.

A task is a grid of three by three cells where the ninth is missing. The rules say how the cells relate: something grows, something is distributed, something is mirrored. On the easiest tasks one rule applies. On the hardest, three or four apply at once, and the options differ in a single detail.

Below are the fifteen rules that change something, each with a solved example. The sixteenth, constant, means that a property stands still in every cell, and it has no page of its own. The examples are the same ones the app shows after a wrong answer.

More about the format: what a matrix reasoning test is and how logical reasoning tests are used in recruitment.

  • Step by step change

    The size increases by 1 step to the right and 1 step downwards.

  • Three values distributed

    Three sets of arrows each appear once in every row and every column.

  • Patterns combined

    The third cell is the first two laid on top of each other. Every line is kept. This holds in rows and columns.

  • Patterns cancelling out

    The third cell is the first two laid on top of each other, but sectors filled in both become empty. This holds in rows and columns.

  • Patterns in common

    The third cell is what the first two have in common: only sectors filled in both are kept. This holds in rows and columns.

  • Arithmetic on the number

    The number of dots on one side of the line in the third cell is the first two added: 1 + 2 = 3. This holds in every row.

  • Movement along a path

    The figure alternates between standing and lying with every step to the right and every step downwards.

  • Mirroring

    The third cell in every row is the first one mirrored in a vertical axis: left and right swap.

  • Rotation of the whole cell

    The whole content of the cell rotates 180 degrees clockwise to the right and 90 degrees downwards.

  • Nested figures

    The inner figure is circle, heptagon and octagon, once in every row and every column.

  • Number of sides

    The number of corners decreases by 1 to the right and increases by 1 downwards.

  • Degree of fill

    The filled share decreases by 25 percentage points to the right and 25 percentage points downwards.

  • Conditional rule

    The fill determines the direction: filled means 90°, unfilled means 270° and dotted means 0°.

  • Covariation

    The number of figures and their size are linked: the more figures, the smaller they are.

  • Slot by slot combination

    The third cell is worked out slot by slot from the first two: two equal symbols become a diamond, a diamond changes nothing, and two different ones among a circle, a flag pointing left and a star become the third. This holds in rows and columns.
