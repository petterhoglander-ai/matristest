Seventeen rule types with solved examples

The rules in a matrix reasoning test.

Every task in a matrix reasoning test is governed by one or more rules. There are not endlessly many; there are eighteen. Anyone who recognises them does not have to search.

A task is a grid of three by three cells where the ninth is missing. The rules say how the cells relate: something grows, something is distributed, something is mirrored. On the easiest tasks one rule applies. On the hardest, three or four apply at once, and the options differ in a single detail.

Below are the seventeen rules that change something, each with a solved example. The eighteenth, constant, means that a property stands still in every cell, and it has no page of its own. The examples are the same ones the app shows after a wrong answer.

  • Step by step change

    The size decreases by 1 step to the right and does not change downwards.

  • Three values distributed

    Three sets of arrows each appear once in every row and every column.

  • Patterns combined

    The third cell is the first two laid on top of each other. Every line is kept. This holds in rows and columns.

  • Patterns cancelling out

    The third cell is the first two laid on top of each other, but lines present in both disappear. This holds in rows and columns.

  • Patterns in common

    The third cell is what the first two have in common: only sectors filled in both are kept. This holds in rows and columns.

  • Arithmetic on the number

    The number of dots on the other side of the line in the third cell is the difference between the first two: 3 − 2 = 1. This holds in every row.

  • Movement along a path

    The figure alternates between standing and lying with every step to the right and every step downwards.

  • Mirroring

    The third cell in every row is the first one with the symbols in reverse order.

  • Rotation of the whole cell

    The whole content of the cell rotates 180 degrees clockwise to the right and 90 degrees downwards.

  • Nested figures

    The inner figure is circle, heptagon and octagon, once in every row and every column.

  • Number of sides

    The number of corners decreases by 1 to the right and 1 downwards.

  • Degree of fill

    The filled share decreases by 25 percentage points to the right and 25 downwards.

  • Conditional rule

    The first symbol determines the second symbol: plus means plus, dot means dot and ring means ring.

  • Covariation

    The number of figures and their size are linked: the more figures, the smaller they are.

  • Slot by slot combination

    The third cell is worked out slot by slot from the first two: two equal symbols become a flag pointing left, a flag pointing left changes nothing, and two different ones among a star, a square and a circle become the third. This holds in rows and columns.

  • Growing step

    The direction rotates clockwise, 45° in the first step and 90° in the next, both to the right and downwards.

  • Turn and change in the cross

    The symbols move 90° counterclockwise with every step to the right and 90° clockwise with every step downwards, and with every step each symbol changes to the next: a flag pointing right becomes a flag pointing left, a flag pointing left becomes a square and a square becomes a flag pointing right.
